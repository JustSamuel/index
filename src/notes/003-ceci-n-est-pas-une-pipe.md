_"Ceci n'est pas une pipe"_ is French for "This is not a pipe". It is most
famously associated with René Magritte's 1929 painting
_The Treachery of Images_, which shows a realistic pipe with that sentence
written beneath it.

Magritte stated that _"if I had written on my picture 'This is a pipe', I'd have
been lying"_, highlighting that the image of the pipe is not an actual pipe
itself. It is merely a representation of a pipe.

Over time, the phrase has taken on a broader meaning in culture: it can be used
to point out the difference between a thing and how it is described or
symbolized.
