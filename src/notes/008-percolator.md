A _percolator_ is a coffee pot that brews by cycling hot water up through a
central tube and letting it drip back down through the grounds. As the water in
the lower chamber heats, it’s pushed upward, splashes over the basket of
grounds, and then drains back down so the cycle can repeat.[^1]

The word comes from the verb _percolate_, meaning to filter or pass through a
porous substance. English borrowed it in the early 1600s from Medieval Latin
_percolare_, which is formed from _per_ (“through”) and _colare_
(“to strain”).[^2] The specific use of _percolator_ for coffee devices
developed in the mid-1800s as inventors began producing pots
based on this repeated filtering action.[^3]

## Why I looked it up

I was curious why this style coffee maker is called a percolator at all and how
far back the term really goes, since it sounds quite futuristic.
