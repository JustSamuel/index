_Cimetière du Père-Lachaise_, in Paris, is the world’s most visited
cemetery. It is the final resting place of notable figures such as
[Pierre Abélard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrus_Abaelardus)
and [Héloïse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9lo%C3%AFse)

Opened in 1801 as _Cimetière de l'Est_ (Cemetery of the East), it was part of
a plan to move burials outside the city walls for public health reasons. At the
time, however, many opposed being buried beyond the city limits, as such sites
were associated with the poor.

To improve its reputation, graves of well-known individuals were relocated
there (e.g. [Molière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moli%C3%A8re) and
[Jean de La Fontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_La_Fontaine)),
cementing Père-Lachaise’s status as both a historical and cultural landmark.
