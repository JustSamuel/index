The adjective _gubernatorial_ means "of or relating to a state governor or the office of state governor." It is most commonly encountered in American English, referring to events such as "gubernatorial elections."

The word originates from the Latin `gubernator`, meaning "a governor" or "steersman." This Latin term itself comes from the Greek `kybernan`, which means "to steer or pilot a ship." The English word "gubernatorial" was formed in the mid-18th century by combining `gubernator` with the suffix `-ial`. While "governor" also stems from `gubernator` via Old French, "gubernatorial" was derived directly from the Latin root.[^1]
