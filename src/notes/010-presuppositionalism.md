Presuppositionalism is a school of Christian apologetics that examines the basic
assumptions people rely on when interpreting the world. It argues that Christian
theism provides the only stable foundation for logic, morality, and rational
inquiry. According to this view, no one approaches evidence from a neutral
position; every worldview operates from its own set of presuppositions. Debates
should therefore focus on revealing and testing these underlying ideas
rather than only exchanging pieces of evidence.

The term comes from the verb _presuppose_, meaning to assume in advance. It
entered English through Medieval Latin and Old French in the fifteenth century.
The suffix _-ism_ marks it as a defined doctrine or method.[^1][^2]
