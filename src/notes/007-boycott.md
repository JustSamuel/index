To _boycott_ means to refuse to buy, use, or participate in something as a form
of protest or social pressure.

The word entered English during the Irish "Land War"[^2] and derives from Captain
Charles Boycott, who managed property for a landlord in County Mayo,
Ireland. In 1880, after Boycott attempted to evict tenants, Irish leader
Charles Stewart Parnell proposed that locals should refuse to deal with
those who took farms from evicted tenants. The tactic was first applied to Boycott himself. Workers stopped working
and local businesses refused to trade with him. Even the postman refused to
deliver his mail.
This action left Boycott unable to hire anyone to harvest his crops.
The word spread quickly through the press and was in common use by November
1880[^1].

Being known as someone who likes etymology, I sometimes get asked if I am
